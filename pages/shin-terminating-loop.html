The terminating loop

The whole build for the price scanner as a single repeating pass with a written exit: what done means, what has to be true at the start of every pass, the full queue in order, and the argument for why it stops instead of running forever.

Why the exit comes first in this document A loop that cannot stop is not a plan, it is a habit. So done gets defined before the work does. Everything here is one of three things: the exit conditions, the pass that repeats until one fires, or the queue the pass consumes. The product design, the build order and the difficulty ranking all fold into that third part.

Done, in three forms, and none of them is "all the features"

The queue holds 68 counted feature options. Emptying it is not the goal and would take years. These are the three states that stop the loop.

Exit one, the good one

Shipped, and they come back without being asked

The floor is live and a real person scanned in week one and scanned again in week two with no notification prompting it. That is the only definition of working that matters, and the bar it has to clear is grim: day thirty retention on iOS averages around 7%.

Exit two, early and cheap

Killed on the price, before any interface exists

No category can produce a defensible verdict often enough. The hand pilot got a usable range for 2 of 7 items from free search, which is why this exit is real and why it is tested in the first pass rather than the tenth. It ends with a finding instead of a product, and it costs one pass.

Exit three, the honest one

Shipped, correct, and nobody watches

The app works and the videos do not travel. Nothing in the build changes this, which is why the video test sits outside the loop and in front of it. 68.07% of apps never reach a thousand downloads and the channel is the only lever on that number.

And done for a single queue item, which is what makes the queue empty

Every item leaves by exactly one of three doors, and all three are terminal. Built, meaning its acceptance test passed and a person checked it where its consumer meets it. Killed, meaning its falsifier fired. Parked, meaning it left with a named condition that would promote it back. There is no fourth state. In particular there is no "in progress" that survives the end of a pass: work that is not finished at the end of a pass goes back to the queue with what was learned attached, which either moves it up or moves it out.

What must be true at the start of every pass

If any of these is false, the pass does not start. Fixing it is the pass.

i
The queue is written down and finite. Nothing is built that is not in it, and nothing enters it without something else leaving.
ii
Every item in it has an acceptance test and a falsifier, both written before work starts. If the falsifier cannot be written, the item is not understood well enough to build, and understanding it is the item.
iii
The scoreboard has a number for last week, produced whether or not anyone looked at it.
iv
Nothing that exited is back without new evidence. Nothing is permanent except the law, so any decision can reopen, but it reopens on a new observation and never on a fresh opinion. This is the single rule that keeps "nothing is final" compatible with finishing.

The rule that stops the loop drifting

Every pass must ship something or kill something. A pass that does neither is a stall. One stall is a bad week. Three stalls in a row means the queue is wrong, not the work, and the next pass is spent rewriting the queue rather than working it.

The pass, six steps, identical every time

One week long, fixed. The length is fixed because an open-ended pass is how a stall hides.

Read the evidence before touching the queue

The scoreboard's number and the defect log come first, in that order, and they are allowed to reorder the queue. Doing this second is the classic inversion: it produces a plan that survives contact with nothing.

Conductor, top tier, reads only

Take the top item and write how it could be wrong

Acceptance test and falsifier, written into the brief before any code. The falsifier is the load-bearing half: the walkthrough labelled its own central claim untested three times in three sections and shipped the label instead of the test. Running it took seven searches and reversed two of the document's decisions.

Conductor writes the brief, worker never writes its own falsifier

Build it

Separate lanes only where the work does not read and write the same things. One worker otherwise. A lane costs its own review and merge, so parallelism that is not real is a pure loss.

Workers, middle tier, one role each, scoped so they cannot touch a sibling's files

Verify with something that did not build it

A fresh process, no project instructions loaded, handed the acceptance test and the change and nothing else. Not a formality: on a spec in another project this exact pattern caught five blockers, two of which the spec itself had introduced.

Fresh verifier per lane, plus one adversary that writes nothing and only tries to produce a wrong verdict

Check it where its consumer meets it

Nothing is done on its own artifact's word. A passing test is not a verdict a shopper accepted. For anything touching price, the consumer-side check is a person holding a phone in a store, and twenty of those fit in one shopping trip.

Founder for anything user-facing, no exceptions and no substitutes

Log the outcome, and let a repeat earn a rule

Built, killed, or parked with its promoting condition. The defect log records whether a test caught it or a human did, which is its only interesting column. A defect logged twice becomes a build standard; once is a bug fix. The alternative was measured here: twenty-eight standing instructions in twenty-two days, most of them backed by nothing.

Conductor, and the log is the only thing the next pass reads first

Pass zero, which is outside the loop because it cannot be redone

Sequenced by what is spent once. A form is refilled in minutes; a name, a store listing and a first video are not.

  • The trademark search. Twenty minutes at CIPO against Nongshim's marks in the software classes. It gates the store listing and it is a risk judgment about a name you will live with.
  • The two-caption video test through borrowed audiences. Three to five micro creators, or a forum where distribution is not follower-gated. Not a new handle: fresh posts seed to existing followers first, so a cold account can return near zero views and hand you a false negative on the one test the whole queue is ordered behind.

Out of pass zero comes a category, a caption and a name, each chosen because something outperformed. The loop then starts. Neither of these can be delegated to an agent, and that is the reason they are the only two items in this document with no agent named against them.

Why this stops

The termination argument, since a plan that loops "until done" is worth exactly what its exit is worth.

  • The queue is finite and counted. 68 build options across 13 tables, plus nine floor rows. It is not a stream and nothing generates new items except a defect that has appeared twice.
  • Every item has three terminal doors and no fourth. Built, killed, or parked with a promoting condition. Parked is not pending.
  • Re-entry costs evidence. Any decision can reopen, and reopening requires a new observation rather than a new argument. Without that clause "nothing is final" and "eventually finished" cannot both be true.
  • Bands 2 through 5 are gated on each other, so a failed cheap version deletes its expensive version from the queue outright rather than deferring it. Eleven of the twelve hard features can be settled this way for days of work each.
  • Stalls are counted. Three passes in a row that neither ship nor kill spends the next pass rewriting the queue.
  • There is a cap on the floor. If band 3 has not shipped in eight passes, the floor was too big and gets cut rather than extended. That number is a judgment, not a benchmark, and the reasoning behind it is that unshipped time is the most expensive thing in this plan: 68.07% of apps never reach a thousand downloads, 0.57% of non-gaming apps ever earn a hundred thousand dollars, and none of that improves while you build.

What would prove this whole document wrong

Three passes in, if the scoreboard has no series and the defect log's last column is all humans, the loop is decoration and every mechanism here failed at once. That is a two-column check and it takes a minute.

The honest confidence split. Pass zero and band 1 rest on a measurement taken by hand and reported in full, failures included. Band 3's lane split rests on band 1's contract holding, which is an assumption until it does. Bands 2 through 5 rest on gates that have never been run. And the loop itself rests on four mechanisms being executed in weeks when nobody is watching, which no process document written in this repo has ever managed. That is why a defect must appear twice before it earns a rule: the loop is designed to stay small, and if it grows it has already failed.

Never in the loop, at any pass

  • The video test. It needs a real audience. The captions can be drafted; the watching cannot be arranged.
  • The store submission. Spent once, and yours. Everything up to the button is the system's to prepare, listing copy and assets included.
  • Twenty verdicts checked against a real shelf. One trip, one hour, and the only check here that cannot be faked by something that wants to report success.
  • The name. Trademark exposure is carried, not searched.
