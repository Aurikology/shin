Correction, 2026-09-03: do not run this test on a new account

TikTok now shows a new video primarily to your existing followers first for the opening days, and only pushes it to non-followers if that seed audience engages fast. Completion and rewatch outrank follower count, which favours small accounts, but the seed still gates the test. A brand new handle has no seed, so it can return near-zero views for reasons unrelated to whether the idea works, which is a false negative on the one test everything else rests on.

Both precedents launched borrowed. Duolingo's account already existed inside a company with an installed base. Cal AI's ladder started with fitness-influencer UGC, meaning other people's audiences, and only later went paid. So: run the test through three to five micro creators in thrifting, deal-hunting or Canadian grocery content at a small flat fee, or post where distribution is not follower-gated (Reddit, where a good post reaches strangers on day one). Run your own handle in parallel to build the seed you will need later, but do not read the result off it.

What the test decides: run the same five scans with two captions, one neutral ("tells you if the price is right") and one with the villain ("tells you if you're getting ripped off"). Read views, rewatch, and how many comments ask what the app is. One week, no engineering, and it settles the brand.

Legal wall, not a preference

Do not put an untested savings figure on this screen. Section 74.01(1)(b) of the Competition Act requires a performance claim be backed by adequate and proper testing done before the claim is made, and it covers in-app and website copy. "People save $1,000 a year" with no users is exactly the exposed case, and the number is suspiciously close to the Dalhousie food price report's projection that a family of four will pay $994.63 more in 2026.

The replacement is better content anyway: a real measured spread with two named stores. "The same four items were $18 apart at two stores 1.6 km from each other" is checkable, specific, and legal.

05

What it thinks it is

Under two seconds to something on screen

Wrong answers are stitchable

The measured limit: across 190 open-source vision models on a 409-product benchmark, the best zero-shot top-1 was 77.0%, while top-5 reached 94.5%. The model usually holds the right answer and often ranks it second. That gap is a design instruction, not a defect.

But asking before the reveal kills the reveal. So resolve it after: show the verdict on the top guess immediately, put the product name in a tappable chip, and open the alternatives only when tapped.

06

Shin's face and two numbers

Must read in three seconds at arm's length

This is where people stay

Yuka's real asset is not the scan, it is the score: one number and one colour that people repeat out loud. Your equivalent is Shin's face. One image per outcome, distinctive enough that a stranger recognises it in a friend's screenshot. Design it hardest, change it least.

This screen has now been tested, and it does not pass as one thing

Version one of this document flagged this stage as load-bearing and untested three times without testing it. So it was run: seven real items, priced by hand from public Canadian sources on 3 September 2026, recording per item whether a defensible range came out. Separately, four retailer product pages were fetched directly. Three returned 403 Forbidden (Loblaws, Best Buy Canada, Metro) and IKEA returned its navigation menu instead of a product. Correction, same day: two of those three failures were the method, not the market

The pilot above concluded that new tech, new furniture and fresh produce were off the shortlist. Asked why, the answer turned out to be three unrelated mechanisms, and only one of them is a real obstacle. The two prices that did not come back had been looked for in the wrong place: search was asked for a live price when a price tracker was the thing to open, which is what a built app would query anyway.

06b

When Shin is wrong and the user knows it

A branch off the verdict, not a step after it

Missing from v1 of this document

Prices are verifiable and consequential, which makes this app different from its two best precedents. A wrong calorie count costs the user nothing they will ever notice, and Cal AI survived persistent accuracy complaints in its own App Store reviews all the way to a $30M year and an acquisition. A wrong price verdict is different: the seller will argue with it, and the user can check it in ten seconds.

So this is the most likely interaction after any bad scan, and it is also the exact moment that builds the one asset a competitor cannot purchase. Handled well it is a data pipeline. Handled badly it is an uninstall.

08

The share, which is the growth engine

One tap, exports a vertical clip

Compounding, if it exists at all

You cannot hand-make enough videos. Paid creative measurably decays, with performance dropping 15 to 20% inside two weeks and average creative life of 10 to 14 days, and TikTok's own tooling pauses fatiguing creative within three to five days. How fast an organic format decays is not something any source measures, which is itself a reason to make supply cheap.

So the app should manufacture the content. Export a 9:16 clip: the item, the reveal timed, Shin's reaction animated, captions burned in.

Take Wordle's counterintuitive detail

Josh Wardle added the emoji grid only after seeing people type them out by hand, and he deliberately left the link out because a link generated a preview that "felt spammy." The share spread further with no attribution than it would have with one. So: Shin, the two prices, your handle small in the corner, and no download link anywhere in the frame.

The paywall, and where it goes

After value, never before

Too early kills the video funnel

Cal AI, built by two 18-year-olds, went from launch in May 2024 to 15 million downloads and roughly $30 million of revenue in 2025, running near $50 million ARR, on a paid subscription behind a free first experience. Yuka went to 80 million users free. Both work. The difference is that Yuka monetises depth and Cal AI monetises the core act.

Cost to plan for: derived from published per-token rates and my own token assumptions, one scan runs roughly $0.008 at the cheapest tier to $0.040 at the top. Window shoppers scan more and buy less, so the bill rises before revenue does. The offsetting fact is structural: window shoppers converge on the same viral items, so a cache keyed on product identity should hit often. Measure the distinct-products-over-total-scans ratio in your first thousand scans. Near 1:1 means no cache and a real cost problem.

12

Week two, and what the app has become

The state worth designing backwards from

If this state exists, the app works

By now the person is not using a price checker. They are browsing with a second opinion in their pocket, and they scan things they have no intention of buying. That behaviour is not marginal: 39% of Gen Z report being constantly in a shopping and browsing mode, against 15% of boomers, and Pinterest built 631 million monthly users on exactly it.

  • They scan four to ten things a week, most of which they will never buy.
  • Their collection has a dozen items in it and functions as a wishlist.
  • They have received at least one price-drop notification and it was correct.
  • They have sent a Shin card to a friend, or posted one.
  • They can name Shin's angry face without opening the app.

Three numbers to instrument on day one, because no benchmark can tell you them and they decide the model: scans per user per week split by whether a purchase followed; watch rate, the share of scans that end in a save; and distinct products over total scans, which tells you whether caching rescues your unit economics or not.

The floor

This document lists roughly 60 feature options across a dozen tables, which makes it a menu rather than a plan. Here is the smallest version that still tests the thesis. Everything else is a decision to make once there are users, which is the only condition under which those decisions get made with evidence instead of argument.

Version one, narrowed by the pilot

One thing still happens before any of it: the borrowed-audience video test. The other precondition, pricing items by hand to find out whether a defensible range can be produced, has been run at seven items and is reported at Stage 06. It came back split by category, which narrows this floor rather than blocking it.

Category
One, chosen from four. New tech, packaged grocery, used marketplace goods, or new furniture. Fresh produce is the only one the pilot ruled out, and it was ruled out on three stacked problems rather than on missing data. New tech is the cheapest of the four to make work and it is where the first attempt at this pilot looked in the wrong place.
Price source
A real feed from day one, never live search. This moved out of "later" on measurement: free search gave a usable range for one item in seven, and three of four retailer pages returned 403 Forbidden to a direct fetch. Cost depends entirely on the category, from an official Best Buy API and a free Amazon.ca tracker at one end, through $0.75 per thousand grocery rows, to Keepa's €49 a month floor at the other.
Verdict shape
One sentence per category, and they are not the same sentence. Grocery: regular price against this week's promotion, never one "typically costs" number, which would have been wrong on the pilot's first item. Tech: against other retailers. Used goods: against live comparable listings. Furniture: against its own price history, because nobody else sells the item.
Input
Barcode and screenshot only. Live photo recognition is out of v1. It is the weakest component at 77% top-1, and the problem statement above deliberately does not require it. Yuka reached 80 million users without it.
Verdict
Three faces plus one honest confidence line in Shin's voice.
Action
Save and watch. One button. The price-drop notification is the return trigger and 4.8 million people already use tools that do only this.
Share
A still card. No rendered video yet. Shin, both prices, no download link in the frame.
Correction
The disagree branch. One tap to fix the item or the price. This is the data pipeline, so it ships in v1 rather than after.
Not in v1
Accounts, paywall, onboarding beyond city, dupes, resale maths, social feed, negotiation drafts, multiple categories, live camera. The price feed used to be on this line and the pilot moved it off.

How to read this document

Stage 06 is load-bearing, and it has now been tested. If a defensible price range cannot be produced often enough, every other screen here is decoration. Seven items were priced by hand on 3 September 2026: a range came out for two, a partial answer for three, and nothing usable for two. Then the two blanks were re-examined and one of them turned out to be a search run against the wrong source, which is recorded as a correction underneath the pilot rather than edited away. Read that stage before this section. It is the only measurement here taken first-hand rather than borrowed, it narrowed the floor below, and it is the one part of this document that changed its own conclusions twice in a day.

Confidence is not uniform. Of the 13 stages, 9 rest on external evidence and 4 are design judgment with nothing behind them: the Shin introduction, the setup question ordering, the adaptive home screen, and the notification triggers beyond Duolingo's measured numbers. Treat those four as guesses. Separately, the grocery figures rest on a real feed at a real price while the furniture and tech stages rest on scrapers that were found but never validated. The pilot did not validate them either; it only established that the free alternative to them does not work.

The priors are unkind and worth holding. 68.07% of apps never reach 1,000 downloads. Of App Store apps published since 2016, 0.95% earned more than $100K, and 0.57% in non-gaming. Day 30 iOS retention averages around 7%. Nothing in this document changes those numbers. The channel is the only thing that does, which is why the Stage 00 correction matters more than any screen here.

The five legal walls, and none of them stops a build

Performance claims. Competition Act s.74.01(1)(b): test before you publish a savings figure. A gate with a key, not a wall. Trademark. The app name, and "the price is right" as a phrase. Personal data. Storing home location and shopping history puts you under PIPEDA; it constrains retention and disclosure, not the feature. Allergen and dietary claims. A food swap suggestion is product liability territory; it constrains the copy. Scraping. Retailer terms are contract and civil exposure, not criminal, and I found no enforcement against Canadian grocery price apps, which means unknown rather than clear.

Figures are labelled at point of use. Benchmark-backed: identification accuracy (190 models, 409 SKUs, arXiv), Yuka and Cal AI scale and accuracy criticism (press, company reports, independent reviews), cart abandonment (Baymard, 50 studies), price knowledge (Dickson and Sawyer, Journal of Marketing 1990: more than half of shoppers could not name the price of the item just placed in their cart), price dispersion (19% average standard deviation for an identical product in one area and quarter), Pinterest and Keepa user counts, creative decay (AppsFlyer), app base rates, Canadian food prices (Dalhousie). Derived by arithmetic: per-scan model cost, from published per-token rates and stated token assumptions. Measured first-hand for this document: the seven-item pricing pilot and the four direct page fetches at Stage 06, run 3 September 2026 against public Canadian sources, reported item by item including the failures. Added to that stage the same day: retailer count at Lowpi, the existence of an official Best Buy developer API and Best Buy Canada's public docs, Keepa's API tiers as reported by a third party because Keepa's own page refuses an automated fetch, camelcamelcamel's free coverage of Amazon.ca, six IKEA price trackers, PLU codes per the International Federation for Produce Standards, and Statistics Canada's monthly fresh fruit and vegetable movements for 2026. Untested: everything about how this specific app will behave, which is what the borrowed-audience video test and the three day-one metrics are for.
