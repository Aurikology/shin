## 3. The four answers

(a) Quebec CPA s.214.3 and the yearly plan. The text prohibits a stipulation renewing a contract "whose term exceeds 60 days" for another fixed term (s.214.3), with a written expiry notice 90 to 60 days out (s.214.4). Pexi's yearly plan is a 12-month term that auto-renews for 12 months, so on its face it matches; the monthly plan (30 days) does not. Whether it applies depends on Division VII (s.214.1) reaching "a service provided at a distance" such as an app subscription; the Act does not define that, Regulation s.79.7 exempts only financial services, lottery subscriptions and travel agents, and the OPC page covers internet-access services only. No source I opened rules either way, and none says whether Pexi or Apple/Google is the "merchant". Conclusion: genuinely unresolved; the text is a live risk for the yearly plan sold to Quebec.

(b) Ontario seller address. Yes, if s.38 applies: O. Reg. 17/05 s.32 item 2 requires the supplier's "telephone number" and "the address of the premises from which the supplier conducts business" before the consumer enters the agreement. Consequences: the consumer may cancel until 7 days after receiving a copy (s.40(1)) and is owed a refund (s.96(1)); a missing s.39 copy gives 30 days from the agreement (s.40(2)). Contravening a regulation is an offence (s.116(2)). Scope: ss.38-40 apply only above a total payment obligation of $50 (s.37, O. Reg. s.31), and Pexi's stated prices are each under $50.

(c) GST/HST and QST. Google: yes. For a Canadian developer without a GST/HST ID or Quebec tax ID, Google "is responsible for" collecting and remitting GST/HST and QST (answer 138000). Apple: by contract, partly. Exhibit C s.3.2(d) says Apple Canada "will charge, collect and remit GST/HST" for a non-registered developer, but that clause also has the developer certify it is not resident in Canada; s.3.2(b) conditions apply only to developers "required to register"; Apple's help page says Canada-based developers are "required to submit" a number. A non-registered Canadian-resident small supplier does not fit Apple's text cleanly. CRA: the Excise Tax Act's platform deemed-supplier rule (s.211.13(1)) covers a "specified non-resident supplier", so the statute itself does not make Apple or Google collect for a Canadian small supplier (s.148, $30,000; RC4022); they do so under their contracts. Revenu Québec: a distribution platform operator outside Québec must collect QST on digital services supplied to Québec consumers "by suppliers outside Québec who are not registered under the general QST system" once its threshold exceeds $30,000. Apple's QST text (Exhibit C s.3.3(a)) requires a Quebec-resident developer to be QST-registered. I did not confirm Apple's or Google's own registration status, or Pexi's province of residence (assumed Ontario).

(d) Charter of the French language. Yes for a Quebec consumer, from these dates. s.50.2: an enterprise offering goods or services to consumers "must respect their right to be informed and served in French", in force 1 June 2022. s.52.1: "All computer software ... must be available in French unless no French version exists", with other languages allowed only if the French version is available on terms "no less favourable" and with equivalent technical features; enacted by 1997 c.24 s.3 (exact in-force day not shown in the consolidation). s.55: a contract of adhesion and its related documents must be in French, and the other party may be bound by a non-French version only after the French version was given and the party expressly wished it; in force 1 June 2023. Pexi has a complete French build but pins English (`languages: false`), so a Quebec user currently gets English only. The Office can also order a digital store operator to stop letting Quebec users contract for non-compliant goods (s.177, fourth paragraph).
