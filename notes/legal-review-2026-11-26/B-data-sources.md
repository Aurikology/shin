Prepared 2026-11-25 for a lawyer to check. This is a fact-and-requirement gap table, not legal advice and not a recommendation. Code is in C:\shin; each fact carries file:line. Each requirement is quoted from the licence or terms page itself, with the URL and clause, read on 2026-11-25. Where a page could not be read, the table says so and says what stands in its place.

## 1. Canadian and US law on facts and compilations

| Point | Authority (primary text read unless marked) |
|---|---|
| A compilation is a work only if it results from "the selection or arrangement of data" | Copyright Act, RSC 1985 c C-42, s.2 "compilation" (b): "a work resulting from the selection or arrangement of data". https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-1.html (read) |
| Compilations are literary works | s.2 "literary work includes tables, computer programs, and compilations of literary works". Same URL (read). Mixed compilations: s.2.1(1), https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/section-2.1.html (read). |
| Originality is required for copyright in a work | s.5(1) (not fetched; cited from the Act's structure, check). Standard: CCH Canadian Ltd v Law Society of Upper Canada, 2004 SCC 13, para 16 (skill and judgment, "not so trivial that it could be characterized as a purely mechanical exercise") and para 25 (a work need not be "creative", novel or unique). NOT RETRIEVED in full text; the SCC and CanLII pages refused the fetch. Wording here is from secondary commentary in search results; verify against the judgment. |
| Compilations of facts, thin protection, Canadian application | Tele-Direct (Publications) Inc v American Business Information Inc, [1998] 2 FC 22 (FCA, 1997). Not retrieved; described from secondary summaries only. Verify. |
| Anti-circumvention | Copyright Act s.41.1(1)(a): "No person shall ... circumvent a technological protection measure within the meaning of paragraph (a) of the definition 'technological protection measure' in section 41". https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/section-41.1.html (read; the s.41 definition was not on the page). Relevant to the User-Agent choices in section 2 (Metro, Consignaction, Return-It). |
| Canada has no separate database right | Stated from general knowledge, not sourced in this review. If correct, Canadian protection for a factual database is copyright in selection/arrangement plus contract (licence terms, site terms). Lawyer to confirm, including enforceability of website terms against a program reading a public page (not researched). |
| US | Feist Publications v Rural Telephone Service, 499 US 340 (1991): "In no event may copyright extend to the facts themselves." and "the copyright in a factual compilation is thin." and copyright "rewards originality, not effort." https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/499/340 (read). Contract and anti-access claims are separate from copyright (Kroger's terms are an Ohio-law contract, Kroger Terms s.10(c)). |

Why it matters here: names, barcodes and prices are facts, so thin or no copyright, but several sources are licensed or termed anyway, and the share-alike and attribution duties are contractual or licence conditions. The practical exposure is contract and licence breach, platform cut-off, and complaint-driven takedown more than a copyright suit over individual facts.

## 3. Requirements from the primary terms

### 3.1 ODbL 1.0 (Open Food Facts family, Open Prices, OpenStreetMap)
Source: https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/ (read; quotes short, rest summarised).
- 4.1: "The rights granted in Section 3 above are expressly made subject to Your complying with the following conditions of use."
- 4.2 Notices: if You Publicly Convey the Database or a Derivative Database, You must use the ODbL, include "a copy of this License (or ... its Uniform Resource Identifier (URI))" with it, and "Keep intact any copyright or Database Right notices and notices that refer to this License." (summarised)
- 4.3 Produced Work: "Creating and Using a Produced Work does not require the notice in Section 4.2", but a Publicly Used Produced Work needs a notice "reasonably calculated to make any Person that uses, views, accesses, interacts with, or is otherwise exposed to the Produced Work aware that Content was obtained from the Database ... and that it is available under this License." (summarised)
- 4.4 Share alike: "Any Derivative Database that You Publicly Use must be only under the terms of:" this licence, a later similar version or a compatible licence.
- 4.5 Limits: internal use is not public use; creating a Produced Work does not create a Derivative Database; a Collective Database need not be ODbL as a whole. (summarised)
- 4.6 Access: if You Publicly Use a Derivative Database or a Produced Work from one, "You must also offer to recipients of the Derivative Database or Produced Work a copy in a machine readable form of:" the Derivative Database, or a file of changes. Internet distribution must be free. (summarised)
- 4.7: no additional terms or technological measures that restrict the rights granted.
- Definitions (1.0): "Publicly" means "to Persons other than You or under Your control"; "Convey" excludes "network interaction" and producing works without transferring a copy; "Derivative Database" includes "putting extracted Contents into a new database". (summarised)

"Individual contents of the database are available under the Database Contents License." "Products images are available under the Creative Commons Attribution ShareAlike licence." Reusers "have to mention the licence and to attribute the authorship to Open Food Facts" and "Derivative works must be shared under the same conditions."
