### Famous-mark issue: ROTO-ROOTER

- US: four live ROTO-ROOTER marks in class 042 appear in the filtered results (serials above), owner ROTO-ROOTER CORPORATION. A web summary of Chemed's SEC filings
  (the parent, since 1980) says the Roto-Rooter marks have been used since 1935 and are "among the most highly recognized" in the US; that is the owner's
  own statement, not a court finding (`https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000019584/000095015296001210/0000950152-96-001210.txt`,
  and `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roto-Rooter`, read as search summaries).
- The rule that a famous mark can block across classes:
  - **US:** 15 U.S.C. 1125(c)(2)(A): "A mark is famous if it is widely recognized by the general consuming public of the United States as a designation of source of
    the goods or services of the mark's owner"; relief against dilution is available "regardless of the presence or absence of actual or likely confusion, of competition,
    or of actual economic injury" (`https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1125`).
  - **Canada:** Trademarks Act s.22(1): "No person shall use a trademark registered by another person in a manner that is likely to have the effect of depreciating the
    value of the goodwill attaching thereto." (`https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/t-13/section-22.html`).
- Whether ROTO alone, used for a grocery price scanner, "dilutes" ROTO-ROOTER (the famous mark is ROTO-ROOTER, not ROTO) is an attorney judgment. This
  search shows the exposure exists; it does not measure it. Roto Frank is a global brand in windows, not a consumer household name, so the fame argument there is weaker; nothing was checked.

## 3. Meaning

- Spanish: "roto" is an adjective meaning "broken", the irregular past participle of "romper" (to break), from Latin "ruptus"
  (`https://www.wiktionary.com/wiki/roto`, `https://www.spanishdict.com/translate/roto`, via a search summary). RAE (`dle.rae.es/roto`) and Merriam-Webster returned HTTP 403 and
  were not read directly.
- Italian: "rotto" means broken, damaged or out of order; past participle of "rompere" (`https://larousse.com/en/dictionaries/italian-english/rotto/34703`,
  `https://dailyitalianwords.com/italian-word-rotto-broken/`, search summaries).
- Fact only, no opinion: French is an official language of Canada, and the US has a large Spanish-speaking market. "Roto" is therefore a word with an established
  meaning to Spanish speakers in both. NOT RUN: whether "roto" has a meaning in French, and any foreign-equivalents analysis.
