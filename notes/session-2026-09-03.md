## Difficulty findings (2026-09-03, third correction of the same shape in one session)
Three features came OFF the hard list by opening a source instead of reasoning:
- **Store-level grocery prices ARE purchasable.** Scraper feeds resolve nearest store from a
  Canadian postal code or take a store id, with normalised $/100g and $/100ml. GroceryPulse.ca
  runs a weekly panel: 50 basket items, 12 cities, 22 banners, ~160 stores, per-observation
  history, licensable. Still open: arbitrary products across all retailers.
- **Video rendering is cheap.** Shotstack $0.20-0.30/rendered min; Remotion $0.01/render above a
  $100/mo floor; Renderly ~$0.10/min. A 15s card is cents. Not infrastructure.
- **Yuka's "alternatives" is NOT a similarity model** - same category, better score. So dupes =
  same category, lower unit price. Easy. The hard part is only the claim "tastes the same"
  (product liability once allergens differ).

**The 12 hard ones collapse into 4 problems:** 6 are one cost shape (anything continuously
re-priced costs per WATCHED ITEM forever, scales with saves not users; derived: 1,000 users x 5
saves x 2 checks/day x 3 retailers = 900k lookups/mo = **$675/mo** at $0.75/1k, before revenue);
3 need the crowd shelf-price asset nobody sells; 2 are legal/business not engineering (savings
claim, brand data); 1 is **"pick Shin's attitude"**, which triples every string forever and is
the most underestimated row in the walkthrough.

## Paths ruled out, and why (do not re-propose without the reversing condition)
- **New-account video test.** TikTok seeds new posts to existing followers first; a cold handle
  can return ~0 views and give a false negative. Use 3-5 micro creators or Reddit instead.
- **"Shin Ramen" as the name.** Nongshim's SHIN RAMYUN is registered (US, first use 1987) and
  "Shin Ramen" is its English-market form. Reverses if a CIPO search clears software classes.
- **A $1,000/yr savings claim.** Competition Act s.74.01(1)(b) needs testing before publication;
  the number also mirrors Dalhousie's "$994.63 MORE in 2026". Reverses once measured.
- **"The Price Is Right"** as name or brand line: protected TV property.
- **Produce as a v1 category. Apparel too** (no model numbers, no comparable set). Used furniture,
  new furniture and new tech are NO LONGER ruled out, see the pilot correction above.
  No *purchasable* feed for produce. NOT no data:
  crowdsourced shelf prices (ScanRebel), receipt OCR (Checkout 51/Fetch/Ibotta), shelf-tag OCR,
  flyer OCR all work, and that layer is the one asset a competitor cannot buy.
- **My own claim that 14 scans/trip is unworkable.** Refuted by Yuka doing exactly that at 80M
  users. Do not reuse the per-trip effort arithmetic as a willingness argument.

**CORRECTION, same day (he asked "why does it not work for new tech, furniture and produce").
Two of the three failures were my method, not the market. Do not repeat the wrong version.**
- **New tech is the BEST-served category of the five.** Lowpi compares 32 Canadian retailers and
  ships as an app; **Best Buy has an official developer API** and Best Buy Canada publishes its
  own docs whose sample datasets include competitor price data; Keepa covers Amazon.ca from
  ~EUR 49/mo; camelcamelcamel covers Amazon.ca free; photoprice.ca does Canadian + cross-border
  for cameras/lenses/computers. My failure: asked search for a live price instead of opening a
  tracker, which is the source class a built app would query.
- **New furniture: data exists** (6+ live IKEA trackers, plus camelcamelcamel for IKEA resold on
  Amazon). Real obstacle is **no second seller** of a POÄNG, so the verdict becomes price vs its
  OWN history, not vs other stores. Valuable because IKEA's rolling promos never show on the
  product page. This is the Keepa move.
- **Produce is the only genuinely hard one,** three stacked: PLU names a category not a product
  (4011 = bananas, since 1990); package formats break unit comparison; and StatCan movement is
  underlying not promotional (fresh fruit +4.7% in one month, largest July since 2011; fresh veg
  +5.5% in one month, largest May since 2008; +7.8% y/y in March). No promo split fixes it, so
  shopper-reported shelf prices are the ONLY source here, not a supplement.
