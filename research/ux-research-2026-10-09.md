## 2. Time to first value / onboarding

- Camera permission: contextual ask raises grant rate; benchmarks are vendor numbers, not studies · https://linkrunner.io/blog/top-10-mobile-app-onboarding-metrics-that-predict-long-term-retention [SNIPPET] · camera/photo grant "70-90%" when requested inline; push "60-80% rejection" when asked at launch
- Pre-permission explainer lifts opt-in · https://www.dolfy.ai/blog/permission-prompt-problem-camera-location-mobile-apps [SNIPPET] · cold camera prompts denied by "about half" of first-time users; primer lifts to "roughly 65-80%" (labelled "commonly cited", no source)
- One case: short contextual line before gallery access gave +40% acceptance · https://edana.ch/?p=52646 [SNIPPET, agency case] 
- iOS system alert is one-shot, so a primer protects it · https://dev.to/pykero/when-to-ask-for-push-notification-permission-and-when-not-to-42a3 [SNIPPET] · "A declined system prompt is hard to recover"
- Apple rules: use the system alert, customise only the purpose string; do not replicate the alert with a fake one · https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-purpose-strings [SNIPPET] · "Ensure your purpose strings clearly and completely describe your use of the data."
- Account wall before value: secondhand number, original unverified · https://trysonar.app/blog/app-onboarding-that-protects-day-1-retention.md [SNIPPET] · "up to 56%" higher Day-1 churn (cites Localytics; original not found)
- Registration friction surveys (general, not a hard wall): https://www.thedrum.com/news/56-don-t-sign-app-because-long-registration [SNIPPET] · 56% would not sign up for an app with long registration (Foolproof 2012, old)
- Yuka, Cal AI and RevenueCat case studies all converge on getting to the aha moment fast · https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-placement.md [FETCHED] · Superwall's Jake Mor: "The most important thing is getting a user to an aha moment as quickly as possible."; cites losing 50% of signups before aha as the case for an earlier paywall
- Install-day concentration of conversions · https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/ [FETCHED] · "Between 78% and 90% of trial starts happen on Day 0"; "Day 0 conversion: 50.6% overall" (the 82% figure seen in blogs is second-hand)
- No controlled evidence found for "scan before sign-up" specifically: UNSOURCED (only general onboarding-friction data above)

## 3. Showing uncertainty to lay users

- A numeric range costs little trust; vague "we are uncertain" wording costs more · PNAS 2020 van der Bles et al., summary at https://blog.aiimpacts.org/p/paper-summary-the-effects-of-communicating [SNIPPET; pnas.org returned 403] · numeric range lowered confidence in the number but "did not reduce their trust in the source"; imprecise verbal statements "can undermine both trust in the numbers and their source"
- Best field format: flag the figure as an estimate AND give the range · BBC online field experiment, via https://www.nuffieldfoundation.org/news/uncertainty-about-facts-can-be-reported-without-damaging-public-trust-in-news [SNIPPET] · raised perceived uncertainty "with little to no drop in trust"
- Mobile, small screen, lay riders: discrete dot displays beat density plots · Kay et al. CHI 2016, https://idl.uw.edu/papers/when-ish-is-my-bus [FETCHED] · quantile dotplots "reduce the variance of probabilistic estimates by ~1.15 times compared to density plots"; "more confident estimation by end-users". Direct caution for a smooth bell curve: a dotplot may read better than a curve (inference)
- Same group, decisions not just estimates · https://idl.uw.edu/papers/uncertainty-bus [SNIPPET] · incentivised study: quantile dotplots "yielded better decisions" than text-only or no uncertainty
- Calibrated probability forecasts raised decision quality and trust (weather) · Joslyn work, review at https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.4812 [SNIPPET] · "providing calibrated probability forecasts improve weather-related decision-making and increases trust in the forecast"
- Lay misreading of probability is common even in a familiar format · https://livescience.com/5407-rainy-weather-forecasts-misunderstood.html [SNIPPET] · only 43% correctly answered "can't tell from this forecast" (Joslyn, college students)
- Inconsistency hurts trust less than inaccuracy; reliable uncertainty estimates amplify decision benefit · Burgeno and Joslyn, https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/items/2606e30e-b9a8-4669-9a3f-57914fff773c/full [SNIPPET]
- "Hollow/wider" encoding of confidence has a published cousin: value-suppressing palettes make high-uncertainty values look less distinct, and people then weighted uncertainty more · Correll, Moritz, Heer CHI 2018, https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/value-suppressing-uncertainty-palettes [SNIPPET, abstract only] · tested on maps, not bell curves; whether a hollow bell reads as "unsure" to a lay user is UNSOURCED, test it
- Adding a point mean to an uncertainty display biases viewers toward discounting the uncertainty (Kale, Kay, Hullman) · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.14516 [SNIPPET] · "small biasing effect"; a face verdict is a point estimate and may swamp the bell
- Error bars are poorly read by lay viewers; hypothetical outcome plots do better (Hullman, Resnick, Adar 2015) · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.07687 [SNIPPET, via a survey] · did not find Padilla, Kay and Hullman review text itself
- Trust when the app is wrong after a stated uncertainty: no consumer-app study found. UNSOURCED. Closest: inconsistency vs inaccuracy result above (weather)

## 4. Mascots in utility apps

- No controlled study isolates Duo's effect on retention · https://www.lazyweb.com/research/duolingo-retention-experiments.md [SNIPPET] · UNSOURCED causal claim; the often-quoted "Journal of Educational Computing Research" mascot study could not be verified
- Duolingo's own guidance: Duo is "helpful, motivating, persistent, and supportive", never threatening, angry or mean · https://design.duolingo.com/writing/duo [SNIPPET]
- Duolingo uses the mascot as a retention lever through reminders (shame works); context is push, which Pexi v1 lacks · https://design.duolingo.com/writing/duo [SNIPPET] · "The shame is real...and very effective for open rates and retention" (second-hand paraphrase of internal guidance)
- Finch (companion pet app): attachment visible in reviews, not proven as retention cause · https://appbot.co/blog/finch-app-reviews-emotional-attachment-user-retention-product-loyalty/ [FETCHED] · 40,294 reviews Aug 2025 to Aug 2026, 4.59 stars, 82.1% positive; 88 of 400 positive reviews mention the bird; "review data shows what users say, not proof that attachment causes retention"
- Finch's risk of attachment: losses feel personal · same URL [FETCHED] · 28 reviews describe lost progress, birds or accounts; pricing tagged in 27% of iOS negative/mixed reviews; "Users can love their bird and still be unhappy with the way they're being charged."
- Pedagogical-agent meta-analyses (learning, not utility): small positive effect, image of the agent not required for affective benefit · https://karlkapp.com/?p=4509 and https://rex.libraries.wsu.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/A-Systematic-Review-of-Pedagogical-Agents/99900601053701842 [SNIPPET] · 43 studies / 3,088 participants, "small but significant"; "agent's image may not be necessary" for affective benefit. No mascot-specific study found
- Clippy: failure attributed to timing and intrusiveness, contested vs anthropomorphism · https://www2.cio.co.nz/article/662045/what-went-wrong-clippy-virtual-assistant-pioneer-people-loved-hate/ [SNIPPET] · Sinofsky: "early and wrong"; designer Alan Cooper argued the mistake was the character itself
- Facial geometry affects trust in humanised characters: narrower-faced mascots trusted more · Marketing ZFP 2019 (found via search, title page not opened) [SNIPPET] · applies to the cartoon face design choice; effect absent for feminised entities
- Duolingo's fake-death stunt: some users said it broke trust, no measured harm found · https://www.sportskeeda.com/pop-culture/news-how-this-netizens-react-duolingo-s-mascot-owl-duo-comes-back-faking-death [SNIPPET] · "post gained over 550,000 likes on X"
- When a mascot hurts trust (inference from above, not directly sourced): a cheerful face on a wrong or low-confidence verdict, and any reminder-style pushiness. Evidence that a face reduces trust in a price verdict specifically: UNSOURCED
- Yuka has no mascot and still leads the category (80M users) · https://www.scandit.com/resources/case-studies/yuka [FETCHED]; Honey-style trust problems stem from incentives, not characters

## Strongest implications (inference, for the decision, not sources)
1. Yuka has no scan cap and no mascot; both of Pexi's distinctive choices lack a closest-analogue precedent.
2. Vivino's 2025 reviews show gating the verdict (rather than the scan) angers users.
3. Numeric range on an estimate costs little trust; vague hedging costs more; dotplots beat density curves on phones.
4. Shelf and app price mismatch is the top Flipp complaint; Pexi's typed shelf price sidesteps it.
5. Hard paywall 10.7% vs freemium 2.1% median, but Pexi's 5-free-scans-a-week is neither; no benchmark exists.
