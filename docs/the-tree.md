# The tree

The root is the moonshot. Going down a level means "what has to exist for the thing above it to
exist". Read a line upward and it says what that piece unlocks; read it downward and it says what
it is waiting on.

**Five levels, 2026-09-06.** The root, the ten things under it, and three layers of what each of
those is made of. The shape, counted rather than claimed: **98, then 315, then 522, then 66**,
with **665 ends** spread across the depths. It fans out where a thing is still made of parts and
stops where it is not, so a level being narrower than the one above it is only correct when the
level above it is mostly ends.

**Why it was redrawn.** The fourth level was 67 lines under 315, which is a tree narrowing as it
descends, and that is the mark of decomposition stopping early rather than of work being simple.
His words, 2026-09-06: *"the levels are too broad. each level is a culmination of multiple,
slightly less complex levels. the fact that the fourth layer has less nodes than the 3rd layer is
a big red flag."* The cause was a bad stopping rule: being able to name a measurement was treated
as reaching the bottom, which stops wherever a line is easy to describe rather than wherever it is
actually small.

**The stopping rule now, and it is three things at once.** A line is an end when one person can do
it in one sitting, when it needs no decision that belongs to somebody else, and when it produces
one thing you can point at. Anything else has children, whatever level it sits on. Drawn from the four documents
only: what the app is for, how it ships, the moonshot, and the fault list. Nothing here is marked
built or not built, because that marking reads the code and opens every citation, and it is a
later pass. A second-level line still says **not decomposed yet** where its own children are the
third level below it rather than the end of the branch.

**A third-level line names where it came from and which figure it moves.** Its kind of item is
its parent's unless the line narrows it. **(one sitting)** on a line is a statement about size
only, and it is not a mark of state.

**"Shown by" is what makes a line an end.** The rules say a buildable end is one pass plus the
measurement that will show it worked, so a line that cannot name one is not finished being
decomposed. Every end names something you could go and look at: something in the running app, on a
real phone, in a real bill, or in front of a person who has not seen the app before. Not a test
suite going green, because a green suite has already once read 39 of 39 in this project while the
thing it covered was wrong; and not a note we wrote ourselves, because a note is the thing's own
word for itself.

**The numbers this document states about itself are counted, not typed.** Running
`node scripts/count-the-tree.mjs` prints them and fails if the prose disagrees with the file. It
exists because the prose was wrong three times in one day, and a reader caught it each time.

**What carries no measurement, exactly.** Nine ends: four calls that are his, and five things
blocked by somebody outside. Neither kind is work, so neither has a measurement; each says instead
what he decides, or what would lift the block. One more line is a pointer at work built elsewhere
and says so. Two end-state definitions carry a measurement for the half that can be shown today
and say plainly which half waits on a wall.

**What this document does and does not claim about state.** No line marks a thing built or not
built; that pass reads the code. Some lines do carry a fact that was measured or looked for on a
date, and those say the date. Both are true, and telling them apart is what the date is for.

**Three words that would otherwise mean four things each.** A *photo reader* is a paid model that
looks at a picture. An *on-phone reader* is the small trained one that would run on the handset.
A *storefront collector* is what reads a shop's own pages. A *person* reading something is said
as a person. The word reader on its own is never used for a collector.

**Two things this tree holds at once.** The end state is the moonshot. The way there is the
shipping design, which deliberately does less: the viewfinder waits, the shutter is the act, the
person types the asking price while the naming runs behind the sheet. Where the two differ, the
line says which one it is describing. The moonshot's continuous live reading of every tag in the
frame is the end state, not the next build, and that is written on the line rather than left for
a session to trip over.

**How to read a line.** Plain words first, then four things: the **kind** of item it is about,
the **goal figure** its subtree moves, where it **comes from**, and its **state**. A number says
whether it was counted, derived, or is an untested target. Cross-references sit in a trailing
bracket, never in the sentence.

**The goal figures**, and a line names one of these and not a category: installs · installs per
video · return in week two · answer rate per kind · scans per returning person per week ·
corrections per hundred verdicts · downloads · payers per hundred downloads · what each payer
leaves after the store's cut and the model bill · savings measured.

**One of those figures cannot move as it is written, so it is split here.** Since it always
answers, the share of scans that return something is fixed at everything, and a line claiming to
move it claims nothing. The figure a line names is therefore either the share answered from real
prices on the thing itself, or the share answered from something weaker down the evidence ladder.
The first is the one worth moving; the second going up is a warning, not a win.

**Six states an end of the tree can have**, and nothing else: running · written with nothing
calling it · buildable in one sitting · blocked by someone outside, with what lifts it · his to
decide, written as the question · a wall, meaning a thing a company our size cannot build, kept
as a slot with its way around underneath it.

## The root

**Every price at every major Canadian chain, in every store, live; every purchase a person makes,
itemised, arriving on its own; and a promise backed by money that a Shin user never pays above
the fair line.**
*Kind: all kinds. Moves: every figure. From: the moonshot, its one sentence, 2026-09-06.*

Ten things below. Nine are the parts; the tenth is the app itself, which is the only one a person
ever touches and the one that joins the others into something openable. If all ten existed,
nothing else would be missing for the sentence above to be true.

## What can be worked at once, and what cannot

Knowing what a thing is, knowing what it costs, and the app itself are independent of each other
at the top and can be worked at the same time. Saying fair or not waits on knowing what it costs.
Knowing what was bought waits on nothing here but its own permission. The money waits on knowing
what was bought for anything that pays out, and on install scale for the seller mark. The public
half of people finding it waits on the name. Not being gamed is a gate rather than a follower: it
