Subject: Comment on the draft Children's Privacy Code, from a small app publisher

To the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada,

I publish Pexi, a free iPhone price-checking app for Canadian grocery shoppers. It is for all ages, it asks for no name,
email, birth date or account, and it is not aimed at children, but children can use it, so I read the draft Code as
applying to it. I have three comments from the point of view of a one-person publisher.

1. **Say what "likely to be accessed by children" asks a small publisher to write down.** I wrote a one-page record
   (the decision, what is collected, why it is necessary, what is off by default, and who answers a parent). A model
   record of that size in the final Code would help publishers who have no lawyer.

2. **Clarify consent where no personal details are collected.** My app uses a random device identifier to count weekly
   scans and keep a short security log. It never asks a child for a name or a contact detail. It would help to know
   whether necessary, non-sensitive data of that kind may rest on implied consent, and what age-appropriate notice the
   OPC expects instead of an age gate that would itself collect a birth date.

3. **Give a start date.** A date long enough after the final Code for a small publisher to change the product and test it
   would be welcome.

Thank you for the chance to comment.

Jamin Xu
Publisher, Pexi
useshinapp@gmail.com
